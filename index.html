How it works

The four-phase model

Phase 01

Locate

Spectroscopy surveys identify high-value near-Earth asteroids with favorable orbits.

Phase 02

Capture

Launch a spacecraft to the selected asteroid and envelop it with a capture bag.

Phase 03

Transport

Move the captured asteroid to cislunar space using fully autonomous AI navigation.

Phase 04

Deliver

Deliver asteroids to mining customers. Astra Retrieval owns the logistics — not the mining and refining.

Our targets

Which asteroids we're after

Of the more than 40,000 near-Earth asteroids catalogued to date, two classes hold the resources the space economy needs most.

C-type

Carbonaceous · most common

Dark, ancient bodies made of clay, silicates, and carbon compounds — among the oldest objects in the solar system. Rich in water and volatiles, making them prime candidates for in-space propellant production.

  • Water
  • Carbon compounds
  • Clay silicates
  • Organic matter

M-type

Metallic

Believed to be the exposed iron-nickel cores of ancient protoplanets shattered by collisions billions of years ago. Near-uniform metal composition throughout — the primary target for platinum-group metal extraction.

  • Iron (~90%)
  • Platinum-group metals
  • Rare earth elements
×13 more platinum in the best asteroids than in the best terrestrial mine ore
×40,000 more platinum in a typical M-type asteroid than in Earth's crust
×15,000 more iridium in M-type asteroids than in Earth's crust

Asteroids as a water resource

Water is the most valuable near-term asteroid resource

NASA's OSIRIS-REx mission found water-bearing clay minerals and carbon-rich organics on Bennu, and JAXA's Hayabusa2 confirmed hydrated silicates on Ryugu. C-type asteroids are roughly 10% water by mass — a single 100 m asteroid could hold on the order of 100 million kg of recoverable water.

  • Rocket propellant & refueling depots
  • Radiation shielding
  • Life support & oxygen
  • Satellite servicing
  • Space habitats & agriculture

Why it matters

Critical minerals, concentrated supply

Platinum-group metals and rare earth elements power the technologies defining the 21st century — yet supply is concentrated in just a few countries.

Platinum-group metals

Pt · Pd · Rh · Ir · Ru · Os

Used in catalytic converters, hydrogen fuel cells, electronics, cancer treatment, and hard disk drives. Annual platinum output is so small it would fit in a living room.

South Africa
72%

of global platinum production

Rare earth elements

Pr · Nd · Tb · Dy · +13 more

Essential for EV motors, wind turbines, smartphones, defense systems, and MRI imaging. Every EV contains roughly 1–3 kg of neodymium.

China
69%

of rare earth mine production — and ~90% of global refining
