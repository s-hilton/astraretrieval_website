Why water

Everyone knows why metals are valuable. Here's why water is the most valuable near-term resource.

Today, almost everything used in space has to be launched from Earth. Water found in space can instead be split into hydrogen and oxygen for rocket propellant, and it supports life and shields people and equipment from radiation. NASA's OSIRIS-REx found water-bearing clay minerals on asteroid Bennu,9 and JAXA's Hayabusa2 confirmed hydrated minerals on Ryugu.10

  • Rocket propellant and refueling depots
  • Life support and oxygen
  • Radiation shielding
  • Satellite servicing
  • Space habitats and agriculture

  • In-house designs for what a capture mission needs, including the capture bag that envelops and cinches around the asteroid

In development

Software

Autonomous deep-space flight

Far from Earth, every command arrives minutes late. Our software lets a mission be simulated in full before launch, then flies the trajectory, rendezvous, and capture on board, adapting in real time as conditions change in flight.

  • Full mission simulation before launch
  • On-board navigation and guidance that adapts in real time
  • Autonomous rendezvous with a tumbling target
  • Mission operations with minimal ground intervention

Mission · Asteroid capture & cislunar return

Own the logistics, not the mining

We focus on capture and transport. Miners and refiners get water-rich and metal-rich asteroids delivered to cislunar space, without flying years into deep space to find them. This reduces capital expenditure and risk for miners, since they don't have to depend on a single mission.

How a mission works

Four steps

Step 1

Locate

Spectroscopy surveys identify high-value near-Earth asteroids with favourable orbits.

Step 2

Capture

The spacecraft rendezvous with the asteroid and envelops it in the capture bag.

Step 3

Transport

Ion propulsion and autonomous navigation carry it to cislunar space.

Step 4

Deliver

Customers mine and refine. We own the logistics, not the processing.

Targets

Which asteroids we're after

More than 40,000 near-Earth asteroids are catalogued.5 Two classes hold what the space economy needs most.

Carbonaceous · most common

C-type

Dark, ancient bodies of clay, silicates, and carbon compounds. Rich in water, the most valuable near-term space resource: propellant, life support, and radiation shielding.

WaterCarbon compoundsClay silicates
Metallic

M-type

Thought to be exposed iron-nickel cores of early protoplanets. The primary target for platinum-group metals and rare earth metals.

Foundation

We design 3D models of the spacecraft and simulate its flight profile on our in-house software. Then we build a physical prototype of the asteroid capture mechanism and test it.

Phase 2 · Year 1 to 2.5

Prove it in space

We launch a prototype of our capture spacecraft into orbit and test its operations in flight.

Phase 3 · Year 2.5 to 10

First retrieval

Our first capture mission flies out to an asteroid, captures it, and brings it home for our first delivery.

Phase 4 · Year 10 onward

Build the fleet

We scale up operations, adding spacecraft until we are returning one asteroid every month.
